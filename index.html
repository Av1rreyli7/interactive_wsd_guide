Know the Warning Signs

A dog almost always tells you it is uncomfortable before it bites. Learn to spot these signs early and you can step away long before there is any trouble.

CalmGetting uneasyAbout to bite
Relaxed“I am comfortable”

  1. Loose, wiggly body

    The body is soft and moves easily.

  2. Soft eyes

    Gentle eyes with no hard stare.

  3. Relaxed mouth

    The mouth is open or loosely closed.

  4. Natural tail

    Held at a normal height, often wagging gently.

A wagging tail is not always friendly

A tail held high and stiff, wagging fast, can be a warning too. Always look at the whole dog.

Even a relaxed dog should be allowed to come to you first.
Uneasy“I am not comfortable”
  1. 01

    Licking its lips

    Quick licks of the lips or nose when there is no food around.

  2. 02

    Yawning

    A big yawn when the dog is not tired.

  3. 03

    Looking away

    Turning its head or whole body away from you.

  4. 04

    Whites of the eyes

    The head turns away but the eyes stay on you, so you see the white part.

  5. 05

    Ears back, tail low

    Ears pressed flat and the tail held low or tucked between the legs.

Give the dog more space now.
Warning“Back off now”
  1. 06

    Stiff, frozen body

    The dog suddenly goes still and tense, often with a hard stare. A bite can follow quickly.

  2. 07

    Raised hair

    The fur stands up along the neck and back.

  3. 08

    Low growl

    A deep rumble from the throat is a clear warning. Never ignore it.

  4. 09

    Showing teeth

    The lips pull back to show the teeth, sometimes with a snarl.

Move away slowly. Do not wait for the next sign.
If you see any of these signs

  1. Stop

    Stop moving and stay calm.

  2. Look away

    Do not stare at the dog.

  3. Turn sideways

    Turn your body slightly to the side.

  4. Back away slowly

    Step back slowly. Never turn and run.

For Kids

Street dogs just want to rest, eat, and be left in peace. How you act around them makes all the difference.

In this chapter
“

Most bites happen because a dog felt scared, cornered, or tricked. None of that is the dog's fault. Learn the rules below and you will almost never have a problem.

Tap any situation to open it.

Avoid03 situations

Situations to Avoid

Put the food down and step back.

Situation

You hold out a biscuit toward a street dog. The dog walks closer. Then you snatch it back or run away laughing.

What happens

The dog feels tricked and confused. It may growl, snap, or bite your hand. Even a normally friendly dog will react this way.

If the dog does this

If the dog growls: freeze completely. Do not pull your hand back fast. Back away slowly with your palm facing down toward the ground.

What to do

Place food on the ground and step back at least one metre. Let the dog walk to it on its own. Never hold food out and then pull it away.

Walk around sleeping dogs quietly.

Situation

You and your friends run toward a sleeping street dog, shouting to startle it for fun.

What happens

The dog wakes up suddenly, scared and disoriented. A frightened dog bites before it thinks.

If the dog does this

If the dog jumps up and barks: stop moving immediately. Do not run. Stand still with arms at your sides and look away.

What to do

Walk around sleeping dogs quietly. Give them at least two metres of space. Never shout at or charge toward a resting dog.

Wait until the dog finishes eating.

Situation

A street dog is eating scraps near a bin. You walk up and try to pet it or reach toward its food.

What happens

Even the friendliest dog will snap when it feels its food is being threatened. This is instinct, not aggression.

If the dog does this

If the dog snaps at you: back away without sudden movements. Do not yell. Give the dog space and it will go back to eating.

What to do

Never approach a dog that is eating, chewing, or drinking. Wait until it finishes and walks away on its own.

Be careful02 situations

Situations to Be Careful In

Look away and turn sideways.

Situation

You stand in front of a street dog and stare directly into its eyes for a long time.

What happens

In dog language, a direct unblinking stare is a challenge or a threat. The dog reads it as aggression.

If the dog does this

If the dog starts growling: keep looking away. Do not back up fast. Stand sideways and take one calm step back at a time.

What to do

If you make eye contact with a street dog, look away slowly and turn your body slightly sideways. Never hold a stare.

Walk slowly. Do not run.

Situation

You walk home alone late through a lane you do not usually use. A group of dogs starts barking and following you.

What happens

Night is when street dogs are most alert and protective. An unfamiliar person walking fast through their space reads as a threat.

If the dog does this

If a dog nips at your heels: stop walking. Turn sideways. Stand still for a few seconds. Many dogs will back off once you stop moving.

What to do

Walk slowly and steadily. Do not run. If they come very close, stop for a few seconds, look slightly away, then continue slowly.

Do this02 moves

The Right Way

Let the dog sniff you first.

Situation

You want to say hello to a street dog that looks calm and friendly.

What happens

You rush toward it and reach over its head to pat it. It flinches and snaps.

If the dog does this

If the dog sniffs and then walks away: that is fine. It said no. Respect that and do not follow it.

What to do

Crouch slightly to one side. Extend your hand low with your palm facing down. Let the dog sniff you first. If it wags and stays relaxed, pet it gently on the side or the shoulder. Never reach over the top of its head.

Stand still like a tree.

Situation

A street dog suddenly charges at you barking loudly.

What happens

You run. The dog chases faster. Running triggers the chase instinct every single time.

If the dog does this

If the dog is still circling after you stand still: take one slow step sideways, not backward. Keep your body sideways to the dog. Avoid eye contact.

What to do

Stop moving the moment a dog charges. Stand still like a tree. Cross your arms. Look away and slightly down. Stay calm and give the dog time to lose interest. Never run.

Chapter 02

For Senior Citizens

Practical, calm knowledge for every morning walk and every market run.

In this chapter
“

For older adults, a dog bite can mean weeks of treatment and disrupted daily life. Every situation below is avoidable. The knowledge takes two minutes to read and will stay with you for life.

Tap any situation to open it.

Avoid03 situations

High Risk Situations

Situation

A dog comes near you on the pavement. Out of fear you raise your walking stick or umbrella high to shoo it away.

What happens

The dog does not know you are scared. It only sees something being raised at it, which reads as an incoming attack. It lunges and bites your arm.

If the dog does this

If the dog is circling you: hold the stick downward and point it loosely toward the ground in the dog's direction. Back away slowly.

What to do

Keep your stick pointed down at all times near a dog. Move it slowly and low to one side, never lift it above your waist. Move your body sideways and walk calmly away.

Situation

You try a new lane on your morning walk at 5am. A group of dogs starts barking and following you aggressively.

What happens

Early morning is when street dogs are most territorial. An unfamiliar person in their lane at that hour triggers a protective response. Walking faster makes them follow harder.

If the dog does this

If a dog is very close behind you: stop walking. Turn your body sideways. Stand still for a few seconds and look away. Many dogs will stop and back off.

What to do

Stick to familiar routes. The dogs there already recognise you. If dogs follow you, slow your pace, face slightly away, and keep walking at a steady speed.

Situation

Two street dogs are fighting near your building gate. Out of kindness you try to step between them.

What happens

In a fight, a dog does not see you as a helper. It sees a third threat and redirects its bite toward you. This is one of the most common ways senior citizens receive serious bites.

If the dog does this

If a dog turns and faces you during or after a fight: do not make eye contact. Back away slowly without turning your back and running.

What to do

Never step between fighting dogs. Walk away and give them space. Dog fights usually end on their own within a short time.

Be careful02 situations

Everyday Situations

Situation

You are walking home from the market with fish or raw meat in an open cloth bag. A dog begins following you closely.

What happens

Dogs can smell raw meat from a great distance. A dog that jumps for the bag can scratch or bite you in the process.

If the dog does this

If a dog grabs at the bag: let go of the outer bag. Your safety matters far more than the bag. Back away and let the dog investigate on its own.

What to do

Always carry raw meat or fish sealed inside a plastic bag, inside a closed cloth bag. If a dog comes close, lower the bag slowly to your side and keep walking without reacting.

Situation

You spot a mother dog resting with her puppies near a doorstep or under a parked vehicle. You walk straight past or bend down to look at the puppies.

What happens

A mother protecting her young is the most protective a dog will ever be. She will bite without much warning if she feels any threat to her puppies.

If the dog does this

If the mother growls at you: freeze, look away, and back up very slowly. Do not turn your back and walk fast.

What to do

Cross to the other side of the road the moment you spot puppies. Do not crouch near them. Give the whole family a gap of at least four metres.

Do this02 habits

Smart Daily Habits

Situation

A dog is sitting or lying on the footpath directly ahead of you.

What happens

You walk straight toward it expecting it to move. It does not move in time and snaps at your ankle.

If the dog does this

If the dog stands up as you pass: slow your pace, look slightly away, keep moving steadily. Do not freeze directly beside it.

What to do

Give any resting dog at least two to three metres of space by stepping wide around it. Walk at a normal steady pace. Do not stare at it as you pass.

Situation

Despite being careful, a dog bites you.

What happens

You panic and wait until the next day to see a doctor. The wound becomes harder to treat.

If the dog does this

Check with the local feeders in your area to find out whether that dog has been vaccinated. Feeders usually know the dogs on their street well and can confirm their vaccination status.

What to do

Wash the wound under running water with soap for at least 15 minutes, as recommended by the World Health Organization. Go to a doctor or hospital the same day without waiting. Start the rabies vaccination course if your doctor advises it.
